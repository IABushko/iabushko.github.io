What Diagnostic Imaging Professionals Do

Radiologic technologists are the photographers of medicine — experts who use X-rays, CT, MRI, and ultrasound to capture images of what is happening inside the human body. They position patients, operate sophisticated imaging equipment, apply radiation safety principles, and produce the diagnostic images that radiologists and physicians rely on to diagnose injuries, cancers, and diseases. They work at the intersection of patient care, advanced technology, and medical science.

Are You a Medical Photographer?

This career could be a great match for you if you:

  • 👀 Enjoy physics and how technology can reveal what we can’t see
  • 🧠 Like solving visual puzzles and spotting tiny details
  • 🤝 Care about helping patients feel calm and safe
  • ⚙️ Are curious about using cool machines with accuracy

Diagnostic imaging professionals are like photographers of the human body. They don’t use regular cameras. They use tools like X-rays, CT, and MRI to capture hidden images inside the body. Every broken bone found, every tumor detected, and every hidden problem revealed starts with someone capturing the right image at the right moment.

School of Health Professions – Diagnostic Imaging

Diagnostic Imaging is the art and science of using imaging equipment to produce images of organs and structures in the human body. The radiologic technologist is responsible for producing many of the diagnostic images that the radiologist uses to diagnose patient injuries and disease processes. The program is designed to prepare students for a challenging career in the Radiologic Sciences through formal didactic and clinical training.

The Program is sponsored by The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center School of Health Professions (SHP) and receives additional support from the Division of Diagnostic Imaging. Students are educated in the institution's state-of-the-art Diagnostic Imaging center and several other affiliated hospitals in the internationally renowned Texas Medical Center and throughout metropolitan Houston. Since its inception in 2005, the program has been successful in graduating highly skilled entry-level technologists.

The first 18 months of study are dedicated to completing the ASRT Radiography Curriculum. Successful completion allows students to sit for the American Registry of Radiologic Technologists (ARRT) credentialing examination in Radiography. Certified students then choose a specialization — CT, CT & Vascular Interventional Radiography, Diagnostic Medical Sonography, MRI, Education, or Management — to complete their bachelor's degree.

● In-person program
● Admits new students Fall semester only
● Full-time enrollment
● Degree completion in 3 years
● Designed for applicants without prior radiologic credentials
● 9% projected job growth by 2030 (BLS)

Competitive Application Process: SHP admits new students once a year for Fall entry. A minimum overall, prerequisite, and prerequisite science GPA of 2.50 on a 4.0 scale is required. Applicants must submit prerequisite coursework, an online application with essay, three professional recommendations, official transcripts from each college attended, and (for international applicants) TOEFL scores plus a course-by-course evaluation of any foreign transcripts.

Estimated Costs (15 credit hours): Texas residents pay ~$2,300 tuition + $650 books & supplies; non-residents ~$8,400 + $650. More than 87% of SHP students receive some form of financial aid. Complete the FAFSA at studentaid.gov using FAFSA code 017249. SHP also offers both guaranteed and competitive scholarships to newly admitted students.
